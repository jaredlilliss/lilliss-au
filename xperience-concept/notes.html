Notes & sources

The apparatus behind the concept: what is quoted, what is argued, where every checkable claim comes from, and what this page is not.

What they say they are not

Unusually, the initiative publishes its own limits. Reproduced here because a concept that quotes an organisation should quote the part that constrains it, not only the part that sells it:

“Not a regulator, government agency or operator of national missions. Not a replacement for UAE institutions or official strategies. Not a speculative venture; programs are tied to measurable readiness and real capabilities.”

That sentence is why the concept page opens both of its place cards by saying UAE Space City and the UAE Space Welcome Center are their proposal, and that the UAE Space Agency runs a separate, real Space Economic Zones programme. A private proposal set in the government’s own design system can be mistaken for a government publication, and the fix is to say so plainly rather than to style it more cautiously.

What is quoted and what is argued

The six stage titles and the two place descriptions are quoted from the initiative’s own published pages. The order between them is not theirs. What has to be true before what is this concept’s argument, and the page says so on screen, above the chain, rather than burying it here.

The four programmes run at the same time. They do not become true at the same time — that difference between a calendar and a dependency is the only thing the concept adds.

On the Arabic

The Arabic is a translation prepared for this concept. Where the initiative’s own published Arabic edition supplies a name, theirs is used — the two pillar names read المشاركة العامة والمجتمع and التعليم ومسار المواهب because that is how they write them. The connecting sentences are this page’s own and are not their published wording.

Typographically: Arabic letters join, so no Arabic run on either page carries CSS letter-spacing — tracking a joined script breaks the words apart. The Arabic face is Noto Kufi Arabic and it leads its own stack, because the space character sits inside Alexandria’s range and a mixed run was otherwise being word-spaced by the Latin font.

Disclosure

This is a concept. It is an unsolicited design study by Jared Lilliss (Lilliss IT), restyling public content from the XperienceSpace Initiative’s own website as a design exercise. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or commissioned by the XperienceSpace Initiative, and it is not published on their behalf. It is not a channel to them: their own site publishes their contact details, and nothing here reaches them.

The figures are theirs. The ordering, the layout and the typography are the proposal. Colours and the two typefaces follow the UAE Design System, which is public.

On the horizon behind the concept page. The UAE Design System’s “solid colours — don’t use gradients” sits under its illustration principles, and it is obeyed there: the eight-pointed khatim is drawn in single solid strokes, geometric and hard-edged. The ground behind it is a gradient on purpose. An earlier edition applied the illustration rule to the sky and became flat printed matter; that is recorded rather than quietly reversed.
