Nobody asked for this page.

Which is the first thing it should say. It is a design concept for XperienceSpace, made in Australia by one person, and sent without invitation. What follows is not an argument that the design is good. It is a demonstration of how the facts on it were handled — because that is the only thing an unsolicited page can honestly offer a reader who has never heard of its author.

Every claim on this page carries its status. There are two, they are never mixed, and the colour means nothing else.

Verified
A named primary source, read and dated. The link goes to the body that published it — not to a summary of it.
Proposed
My idea. Not a fact, not a plan, and not anything anyone has agreed to.

What actually connects your country and mine

An Australian writing to a UAE reader should say plainly what the relationship between the two actually is, rather than assembling one out of adjacent facts. Here it is, in full — four instruments, each dated and sourced, and not one of them a joint programme.

Verified NASA, first signings ↗
“The extraction of space resources does not inherently constitute national appropriation under Article II of the Outer Space Treaty.” Artemis Accords, Section 10 — Space Resources. The provision both countries signed, quoted from the signed text. The signed Accords (PDF) ↗

The Accords are the widest of the four, and the other three are more specific. Two governments agreed to cooperate, an Australian organisation did actual work here, and a trade agreement came into force.

Verified

2019 — the two space agencies signed

The Australian Space Agency and the UAE Space Agency signed a Memorandum of Understanding on 14 February 2019, at Lot Fourteen in Adelaide. It was the Australian agency’s fourth, and covers exchange of best practice and academic and research capability. Australian Space Agency ↗ Dept. of Industry ↗

Verified

Australian work, delivered here

FrontierSI, an Australian geospatial organisation, designed the UAE’s National Geodetic Reference Frame programme for the Federal Geographic Information Centre — governance, infrastructure, standards and capacity building, aligned to UN-GGIM. Phase one computed a national reference frame from 81 continuously operating reference stations across all seven emirates, aligned to ITRF2020. FrontierSI ↗

Verified

2025 — a trade agreement in force

The Australia–UAE Comprehensive Economic Partnership Agreement entered into force on 1 October 2025. No tariff figures appear here: the percentages in circulation reached me through a source whose numbers were corrupted in export, and a figure you cannot read is not a figure you may reconstruct. DFAT ↗

Four instruments, and not one of them is a joint programme. They document two agreements to cooperate, one piece of work delivered, and one trade relationship — not a shared mission, and not a space partnership anyone has announced. So the case for UAE–Australia cooperation in space remains a case someone should make, and this page still does not make it. What I offer below is design work, which is mine to offer.

What is true, with the owner named

Every line below belongs to the UAE government or one of its agencies. Each links to that body’s own publication. Where a fact has changed since it was published, the change is stated rather than quietly dropped — which is the part most decks get wrong, and the part a reader who knows the programme notices first.

Verified

National Space Strategy 2030

Approved by the UAE Cabinet on 11 March 2019. u.ae ↗ UAE Cabinet ↗

Verified

Hope Probe

MBRSC-led. Launched 19 July 2020, entered Mars orbit 9 February 2021 — the first Arab country, and the fifth country, to reach Mars. MBRSC ↗

Verified

Rashid 1 and Rashid 2

Rashid 1 was lost with the HAKUTO-R lander; NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter imaged at least four pieces of debris at the site on 26 April 2023. Rashid 2 is in development at MBRSC. The loss is stated because a page that lists only the successes is not a record. NASA ↗ MBRSC ↗

Verified

Emirates Mission to the Asteroid Belt

UAE Space Agency with LASP at CU Boulder. Launch 2028, first main-belt flyby 2030, seven main-belt asteroids observed, and a final landing on an asteroid in 2033. No target asteroid is named here: the names in circulation appear on no UAE government page. u.ae ↗

Verified

Lunar Gateway airlock

MBRSC agreed on 7 January 2024 to provide Gateway’s Crew and Science Airlock, with NASA flying a UAE astronaut. The programme’s status has since changed: the FY26 budget ended it and FY27 requests no discretionary funding. So this page states the agreement and its date, not an ongoing contribution. NASA, 2024 ↗ NASA, FY26 ↗

Verified

The space economic zone

The UAE’s space SEZ is the UAE Space Agency’s Space Economic Zones Programme, first zone at Masdar City, announced January 2022. “UAE Space City” on the sibling concept page is one of the concept programmes proposed by XperienceSpace, and is not this programme. Masdar ↗

What I would propose, marked as mine

Three things, and every one of them is an offer rather than a finding. None has been discussed with anyone, and the page says so in the same typeface it uses for everything else.

Proposed

An Arabic edition that you write, not I

The right-to-left layout is already built and working on the sibling page, and the Arabic copy is deliberately absent, because it is not mine to write. A page in your language written by someone who does not have it is not respect, it is decoration — and an earlier version of this concept was scrapped for carrying invented graphics with garbled Arabic on them. Numerals stay Western, which is what UAE institutions publish.

Proposed

A sources panel as a permanent fixture

Not a one-off appendix. Every public claim carries its owner and its date, and a check refuses to publish the page if a citation stops matching its record. That check exists and runs against this page; it is the reason the Gateway line above reads the way it does.

Proposed

A structural line between state programmes and initiative proposals

Kept by the layout rather than by a disclaimer paragraph nobody reads. An initiative that describes national missions alongside its own ideas, in one voice, reads as a claim on them — and the fix is not a longer footer, it is putting the two in visibly different places. These are concept programmes proposed by XperienceSpace; they are not UAE government programmes.

What I removed, and why

A research pass supplied a great deal of material about the UAE space sector. Most of it was accurate. These are the things that were not, and they were taken off before this page existed.

This section is here because it is the only part of an unsolicited page that cannot be faked. Anyone can publish claims; publishing the ones you threw away, with reasons, is checkable by the one reader who would know.

Removed

A landmark 2025 aurora observation at Mars

A vivid account of an EMUS observation coinciding with a NASA spacecraft pass, described as definitive proof of a mechanism. No such publication could be found. The most recent real paper covers observations to 2023, poses its central claim as a question, and says more work is needed.

Removed

A named legal instrument on space resources

Cited by number, with articles granting property rights. It appears on no UAE government domain. A structurally plausible citation that resolves to nothing is worse than no citation, because it survives a skim.

Removed

A named target asteroid, and a later landing year

Both circulate widely in secondary coverage. The UAE government’s own page names no target and gives 2033. Where a secondary source and the owner disagree, the owner wins.

Removed

A navigation toolkit described as the UAE’s

Real software, genuinely built for a UAE mission — by a laboratory at a United States university. Right fact, wrong owner, which is the failure this whole page is arranged to prevent.

Removed

A headline valuation attached to the wrong event

A figure belonging to a 2023 merger announcement, presented against the 2024 completion, at which the company was valued lower. Two real facts welded into one false one.

Removed

A financial year presented as evidence of strength

The individual figures were exact; the framing turned four consecutive declines into resilience. If a down year is cited at all, the declines belong in the same sentence. No company financials appear on this page, because the primary sources for them refuse automated reading and reached me only through syndication — good enough for a working note, not for a public page about someone else’s industry.

If any of this is useful

There is no form on this page and nothing is submitted from it. It writes to its author, with the subject already filled in.
